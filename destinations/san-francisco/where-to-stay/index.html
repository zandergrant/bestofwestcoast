California · San Francisco

Where to stay in San Francisco

Union Square for the hotels and the train. The Wharf if the ferry is the morning. A neighborhood if dinner matters more than the lobby.

San Francisco’s lodging choice is a hill chart and a transit chart. Union Square has the hotels and the cable-car turnaround. The Wharf is flat and next to the Alcatraz ferry. North Beach and the Mission are where you eat. Nob Hill is the steep classic. The Richmond and the Sunset, on SF Travel’s own quick facts, are where the fog hangs around. None of that is a star rating. It is a question of which walk you want to repeat.

This guide does not name hotels. SF Travel’s neighborhood pages describe where the rooms tend to be. A named property needs that hotel’s own site, which is a different guide.

Union Square, if you want the most options

SF Travel calls Union Square the main hotel district and a transit hub for Muni, BART, and the cable cars. The square itself is a 2.6-acre park in the middle of the downtown shopping streets. Powell and Market, where two cable-car lines start, is at the edge of this area. BART’s downtown stations are the airport connection: about 30 minutes from SFO on a Yellow or Red line train.

Suits a first visit, a trip that uses BART from the airport, and anyone who wants a lot of hotels to choose from. SF Travel also says this is one of the neighborhoods where visitors looking for lower rates tend to look, along with the Wharf. Trade-off: it is a downtown shopping district. You are not in a neighborhood of the kind the Mission or North Beach is. The hills start as soon as you walk north toward Nob Hill. Cable cars help. They are not wheelchair accessible, so if that matters, this base is still good because Muni and BART are here, not because of the cable car.

Fisherman’s Wharf, if the ferry is the morning

SF Travel treats the Wharf as the part of town visitors expect. Pier 33, the Alcatraz landing, is on the Embarcadero just south of Bay Street. The F-line streetcar runs past the Ferry Building and out to the Wharf. Powell-Hyde and Powell-Mason end here. SF Travel also notes that neighborhoods along the water tend to be flatter than the ones inland.

The National Park Service says there is no parking at the Alcatraz landing, and that meters in the Wharf area are often too short for a visit to the island. A room here does not solve that. Transit does.

Suits a trip with an Alcatraz ticket, families who want the waterfront, and people who do not want to climb. Trade-off: this is the most visitor-facing ground in the city. Dinner can feel like an attraction. The Mission’s restaurants and the bridge are both a ride away. Stay here when the Wharf is half the trip, not when you are trying to avoid it.

North Beach and Chinatown, for food and a walk

North Beach is the old Italian neighborhood. SF Travel is blunt that there is no beach. There are restaurants and cafes, and Chinatown is the neighbor the city’s guide pairs with dim sum. Both sit between downtown and the Wharf, which means you can walk to either if the hill that day is acceptable. The California cable car and the Powell lines are the rides that cross this part of the city. SFMTA lists both Chinatown and North Beach on the Powell-Hyde route.

Suits people who care more about where they eat than about a hotel lobby, and who will walk or ride a cable car. Trade-off: this is not where SF Travel says most of the major hotels are. You may be in a smaller inn or a rental. You are also farther from Golden Gate Park and from the Mission. Do not stay here for a trip that is mostly the park and the west side.

Nob Hill, if you want the classic hotel hill

SF Travel points to Nob Hill for a stay built around grand hotels, dining, and views, and it notes that the cable cars in the old song are climbing this hill. The California line runs from California and Market out to Van Ness. Powell-Hyde climbs through the hill on its way from Market to the Wharf.

Suits a trip that wants that hill on purpose: the architecture, the views, one good dinner. Trade-off: it is steep. SF Travel’s flatter-walk list is the waterfront, downtown, SoMa, the Marina, the Sunset, and the Richmond, not Nob Hill. If someone in the group does not want a climb after dinner, pick Union Square or the Wharf and visit the hill once, by cable car.

The Marina, if you want the bay and a flat walk

SF Travel describes the Marina, on the northern shore, as bars, boutiques, and bay views, and it notes the Palace of Fine Arts, built for the 1915 Panama-Pacific Exposition. It is one of the flatter neighborhoods. The bridge is to the west, which makes this a reasonable base if a bridge walk is the main outing and you would rather be near the water than near Union Square.

Suits a bay walk and a younger nightlife scene, in the bureau’s description. Trade-off: you are off BART. The airport train lands you downtown, and you still have to cross town. Alcatraz is a waterfront ride or a long walk around, not a block away. Fog is more of a Richmond and Sunset story in SF Travel’s notes, but the Marina is still the bay, and the bridge walk is windy on its own schedule.

SoMa, for museums or a ballpark

SF Travel describes SoMa, and Yerba Buena inside it, as museums, venues, and the ballpark. It also puts the larger nightclubs here. Downtown BART stations are the nearby airport link.

Suits a game, a convention, or a museum day. Trade-off: the blocks are bigger and the street life is less like North Beach or the Mission. Stay here when the venue is the reason. Stay in the Mission or North Beach when the venue is one evening and the rest of the trip is walking.

The Mission, if dinner is the point

SF Travel calls the Mission one of the city’s current neighborhoods for restaurants, shops, and murals, and names Clarion Alley and Balmy Alley. Mission, Valencia, and 24th are the streets it distinguishes. The quick-facts table also lists it, with North Beach and the Castro, for bars.

Suits a longer stay, or a first stay for people who would rather eat here than sleep next to the Wharf. Trade-off: the bridge, the cable cars, and Alcatraz are all a ride. You will do transit on purpose every morning you have a waterfront plan. That is fine for three nights. It is a lot for one night before a ferry.

The Castro, if you want that neighborhood and a streetcar

SF Travel calls the Castro one of the country’s first gay neighborhoods and says a trip is incomplete without it, whatever your orientation. The F-line starts in the Castro and runs down Market to the Ferry Building and the Wharf, so the streetcar is the actual link, not a slogan.

Suits people who want that neighborhood as the base and will ride the F-line for the waterfront day. Trade-off: you are not in the hotel cluster, and Nob Hill’s climb is in the other direction. BART from the airport still drops you downtown first.

A fog note, if you are choosing the west side

SF Travel says the city’s fog tends to sit on the Richmond and the Sunset, and that from the Castro you can often watch it come over Twin Peaks. A west-side room is the right base for Golden Gate Park and Ocean Beach, and the wrong base for a first trip that is all Alcatraz and the Wharf. This guide does not try to cover the Richmond and the Sunset in full. The fog note is the part that changes a booking.
