California · San Francisco

Best things to do in San Francisco

Book the ferry or plan the bridge walk. Do not pretend they fit in the same morning.

San Francisco is small and steep, and the first decisions are about tickets and sidewalks, not about finding enough to do. Alcatraz is a ferry you book. The bridge is a walk with hours and a sidewalk rule. The cable cars are a ride that does not take wheelchairs. The neighborhoods fill whatever day those three do not.

The Golden Gate Bridge

The bridge is on US 101, between San Francisco and Marin. It does not have a street address. The bridge district says parking at the visitor areas is extremely limited and tells people to take transit, a tour bus, or a rideshare instead of hunting for a spot.

People on foot, including people using wheelchairs, may use only the east sidewalk, the side facing the city. Hours change with the clock. In Pacific Standard Time, roughly early November to early March, that sidewalk is open 5 a.m. to 6:30 p.m. In Pacific Daylight Time, roughly early March to early November, it is open 5 a.m. to 9 p.m. Construction on the suicide-deterrent system can close it an hour early on some weeknights. Bikes follow a different sidewalk schedule and can cross overnight through a gate buzzer. Dogs are not allowed on the sidewalks, except service animals. Scooters, skateboards, and drones are not allowed. The Welcome Center, on the visiting page, is listed as open daily 9 a.m. to 6 p.m.

Both ends of the bridge meet the Golden Gate National Recreation Area. The district points walkers at those trails rather than at a single must-do path.

Suits a clear day and anyone who wants the walk more than a museum. Trade-off: wind, a sidewalk shared with bikes, and parking that the district itself says not to count on. Check which sidewalk is open for bikes so you are not surprised, and do not bring a dog.

Alcatraz

Alcatraz Island is part of the Golden Gate National Recreation Area. The National Park Service says to plan a few hours for the exhibits, films, and audio tour. Every visit is by ferry, run by Alcatraz City Cruises under contract with the park. The landing is Pier 33 on the Embarcadero, south of Bay Street. There is no food on the island beyond snacks at the bookstore. The park says the weather turns, and to bring a layer and shoes with grip, not sandals.

There is no parking at the landing. Street meters nearby are often too short for the visit. The park recommends transit. The F-line historic streetcars pass the landing. There is a small accessible lot at Pier 33 for people with a disabled placard, first come, first served.

Tickets, fees, and the day’s sailings are on the park’s plan-your-visit page and the ferry company. Buy them before you build the day. This guide does not claim they sell out; it does say you cannot get to the island any other way.

Suits a half day you planned in advance. Trade-off: the ferry time owns the morning or the afternoon. Do not also plan a bridge walk in the same half day. If you have no ticket, do the bridge or a neighborhood and stop refreshing the ferry page from the sidewalk.

Cable cars

SFMTA runs three lines. Powell-Hyde and Powell-Mason start at Powell and Market and run to the Fisherman’s Wharf area. The California line starts at California and Market and runs to Van Ness. SFMTA lists the Powell-Hyde line as operating 7 a.m. to 11 p.m. daily; confirm the other lines and any stop closures on the same site, because construction moves stops.

From 8 a.m. to 8 p.m. you have to pay before you board at Powell and Market, Bay and Taylor, and Hyde and Beach. Fares are on the SFMTA fares page. Single-ride prices are not copied here. Clipper and the MuniMobile app are the ways SFMTA suggests you skip the line. Cable cars are not set up for accessible boarding. Hold on, wait until the car stops before you step off, and do not treat the green X signal as a walk light. It is the signal for the cable car. Full-size bikes are not allowed on board.

Powell-Hyde is the line that climbs through Nob Hill and Russian Hill toward Hyde and Beach. SFMTA lists Chinatown, downtown, Nob Hill, North Beach, and Russian Hill among the neighborhoods it passes. That is why a single ride can replace a lot of steep walking, and why it is a bad plan as your only transit if someone in the group cannot board.

Suits one ride, usually Powell-Hyde, as a way across the hill between Union Square and the Wharf. Trade-off: the wait at Powell and Market, the fare, and the fact that it is not an accessible vehicle. Take it once. Use Muni or your feet after that.

Fisherman’s Wharf, Chinatown, and North Beach

SF Travel describes Fisherman’s Wharf as the part of the city visitors picture first: Jefferson Street, Pier 39, the sea lions, and the ships at Hyde Street Pier. The F-line runs from the Castro along Market and the Embarcadero, past the Ferry Building, and out to the Wharf. Powell-Hyde and Powell-Mason also end in this area. Pier 33, for Alcatraz, is on that same waterfront.

Suits the day you already have an Alcatraz ticket, or a first walk to see the waterfront. Trade-off: it is the most visitor-heavy ground in the city. Eat one meal if you want the setting. Do not spend the only dinner of the trip here unless the water is the point.

North Beach, just inland, is the Italian neighborhood. SF Travel notes that it does not have a beach. It has restaurants and cafes, and it sits against Chinatown. The city’s neighborhood guide sends people to Chinatown for dim sum and to North Beach for that dining street. They are a walk from each other and from the Wharf, with a hill as you move toward Telegraph Hill.

Suits a meal that is not on Jefferson Street. Trade-off: the hills, and less of the flat waterfront walk. Pair them with the Wharf, not with a Golden Gate Park day.

The Mission, for a different city

SF Travel describes the Mission as restaurants, shops, and the city’s best-known murals, and it names Clarion Alley and Balmy Alley. Mission Street, Valencia Street, and 24th Street are the three commercial streets the bureau distinguishes. Nightlife is one of the reasons the same guide mentions the Mission, the Castro, and North Beach together.

Suits a day that is about food and walking, away from the Wharf. Trade-off: you are not next to Alcatraz or the bridge. Do this on the day you do not already have a ferry time. The F-line does not run down Valencia; it does run out of the Castro, which is the next neighborhood west if you want a streetcar back toward the Wharf.

From the airport

BART says trains from San Francisco International Airport reach downtown in about 30 minutes. The station is in the International Terminal, with the AirTrain from the other terminals. Yellow Line trains toward Antioch and Red Line trains toward Richmond both run from SFO into the downtown stations. You can tap a contactless card. BART no longer sells paper tickets. The airport page tells anyone with an early Sunday flight not to count on BART, because Sunday morning service starts later than on weekdays. Check the trip planner for the first train on your day.
