California · San Francisco

A weekend in San Francisco

Give the ferry its own half day. Give the bridge its own half day. Use what is left for one neighborhood.

A San Francisco weekend fails when Alcatraz, the bridge, and the Mission are all booked for the same morning. Give the ferry its own half day. Give the bridge its own half day. Use the third day for a neighborhood. If you only have two days, drop the neighborhood or the bridge, not the thing you already paid for.

Sleep downtown, at the Wharf, or in North Beach if this is the first visit. The Mission and the Castro are better when you already know you want those streets at night. That choice is in where to stay in San Francisco. The rules for each stop are in things to do.

From SFO

BART from the International Terminal is about 30 minutes to downtown on a Yellow or Red line train. Tap a contactless card. There are no paper tickets. Sunday morning service starts later than weekday service, and BART’s airport page says not to plan on the train for an early Sunday flight. If that is your departure, leave the bridge walk off the last morning.

Day 1: one hill, the Wharf, and a real dinner

  • Start at Powell and Market if you want a cable car. Powell-Hyde is the line over Nob Hill and Russian Hill to Hyde and Beach. SFMTA lists it from 7 a.m. to 11 p.m. From 8 a.m. to 8 p.m. you pay before you board at the turnaround. The cars are not accessible. If that rules them out, take the F-line, which runs on Market and the Embarcadero to the Wharf and is the more useful all-day vehicle anyway.
  • Get off for Chinatown or North Beach if lunch is the point. North Beach is the Italian restaurant neighborhood. It does not have a beach. Chinatown is the next street over, and SF Travel sends people there for dim sum.
  • Fisherman’s Wharf and Pier 39 are the waterfront end of the same day: sea lions, the ships, a flat walk. Have one thing here, not the whole afternoon, unless you like it.
  • Do not put Alcatraz on this day unless the only ferry you could get is today. If it is, the Wharf is already where you are, and the rest of the list shrinks to a coffee and the boat.

Day 2: the ferry or the bridge, not both

Pick the one you can actually do.

Alcatraz

You need a ferry ticket through Alcatraz City Cruises, the park’s concessioner, from Pier 33. Plan a few hours on the island. There is no real food, so eat first. Bring a layer. There is no parking at the landing; take the F-line. Meters in the area are often shorter than the visit. When you get back, the day is mostly over. A short Wharf walk is enough. Do not start for the bridge.

The bridge, if you have no ferry ticket

Walk the east sidewalk only. It faces the city. In daylight time it is open until 9 p.m.; in standard time it closes at 6:30 p.m. Those dates shift in early March and early November, and construction can close the sidewalk an hour early. Parking is a bad plan. Use transit or a rideshare. Dogs, other than service animals, are not allowed. Bikes use a different sidewalk at different hours, so expect to share the path. The Welcome Center is listed as open daily from 9 a.m. to 6 p.m.

If the group does not want the full crossing, the view from the San Francisco side still counts. Do not also promise Kerry-style extra viewpoints you have not looked up. The bridge district points people at the national recreation area trails at either end if you want more walking.

Day 3: a neighborhood that is not the Wharf

The Mission is the clean third day. SF Travel singles out the murals in Clarion Alley and Balmy Alley, and the restaurant streets on Mission, Valencia, and 24th. Go for a long lunch and a walk. You are done being a waterfront visitor.

The Castro is the alternative, and it has a practical transit ending: the F-line back down Market if you still need the Ferry Building or the Wharf. SF Travel describes it as one of the country’s first gay neighborhoods. Go for the neighborhood, not for a checklist.

Skip a Golden Gate Park day unless you have already dropped something else. This guide does not lay out the park’s museums. Adding them on the morning you fly out is how you miss BART.

If you only have two days

Day 1 without the cable-car wait if the line is long: walk or take the F-line, eat in North Beach, see the Wharf briefly. Day 2 is whichever of Alcatraz or the bridge you arranged. If you arranged neither, do the bridge in the morning and the Mission in the afternoon. You will have seen the thing the city is famous for and one neighborhood that is not selling you a sweatshirt.

What to leave off this weekend

Lombard Street’s crooked block, Coit Tower, and the Painted Ladies are real places this weekend does not depend on. They were not the stops checked in detail for these hours and access notes, so they are not written up as if they were. If you want one of them, look up that site’s own page and swap it for the Wharf hour, not for the ferry.
