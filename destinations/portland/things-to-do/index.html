Oregon · Portland

Best things to do in Portland

A downtown day on foot, and a park day by train. The two do not fit in one morning.

You can see a lot of Portland without a car if you stay near the center and ride TriMet. The hard part is Washington Park. The rose garden, the Japanese Garden, and the zoo are up there together, and the city says parking is paid and very limited on warm, sunny weekends. The MAX Red and Blue lines stop at Washington Park station. A free seasonal shuttle, and TriMet Line 63, connect the station to the gardens. Explore Washington Park posts the current shuttle and parking notes.

Downtown is the other half of a first trip: shorter blocks than in most American cities, a riverfront park, the art museum, and Powell’s. Pick one park day and one downtown day. Trying to do both, plus Forest Park, is how a weekend turns into time on a bus.

Downtown, on foot

Travel Portland describes downtown as walkable, with the Portland Streetcar for when you want to stop walking. The streetcar runs through downtown, the Pearl, Northwest 23rd, the Convention Center, and out to OMSI.

Pioneer Courthouse Square

A full downtown block, about 40,000 square feet, and a MAX stop. Travel Portland calls it Portland’s living room. It is the useful pause between the museum and the river. There is nothing to book.

Portland Art Museum

1219 SW Park Avenue, on the South Park Blocks. The Oregon Historical Society and Portland State University sit along the same green strip. The museum lists hours of 10 a.m. to 5 p.m. Sunday and Tuesday through Saturday, and closed Monday. Admission is not printed here; use the museum’s site.

Suits a rainy afternoon in the center. Trade-off: Monday is a closed day, so do not leave it for the end of a long weekend that includes Monday.

Tom McCall Waterfront Park and the Eastbank Esplanade

Tom McCall Waterfront Park is 1.5 miles of park along the west bank of the Willamette through downtown. Travel Portland points to Salmon Street Springs, the Japanese American Historical Plaza, and summer festivals. On the east bank, the Vera Katz Eastbank Esplanade runs 1.5 miles from the Hawthorne Bridge to the Steel Bridge and connects back across to the waterfront park. Walk or bike either side. The east side makes more sense if you are already at OMSI.

Powell’s City of Books

The flagship fills a city block at 1005 W Burnside Street, where downtown meets the Pearl. Powell’s describes four floors and more than half a million books. The locations page lists regular hours of 10 a.m. to 9 p.m. daily. A smaller Powell’s at 3723 SE Hawthorne Boulevard lists the same regular hours, which matters if you are staying on that street instead.

Suits anyone who wants one long indoor stop with no timed ticket. Trade-off: Burnside is a wide, busy street, and the store is a store, not a quiet reading room.

Washington Park

Portland Parks describes this as the park that holds the Oregon Zoo, the International Rose Test Garden, the Portland Japanese Garden, Hoyt Arboretum, and the World Forestry Center. Park hours are 5 a.m. to 10 p.m., and the park is closed to vehicles at 10 p.m. Dogs must be leashed. Go by MAX if you can.

International Rose Test Garden

400 SW Kingston Avenue. Portland Parks counts more than 10,000 rose bushes and over 610 varieties, in bloom from late May through October, depending on the weather. A city brochure says admission is free. The parks page lists garden hours of 5 a.m. to 10 p.m., with a closure from 5 a.m. to 10 a.m. every other Wednesday from May through August. An accessible route runs from the parking lot along the main promenade.

Suits a first visit in the blooming months, especially if you want a view over the city without a ticket. Trade-off: outside that season it is still a formal garden, just not the reason to cross town. The parking, not the roses, is what makes the visit annoying on a sunny Saturday.

Portland Japanese Garden

611 SW Kingston Avenue, beside the rose garden and not included with it. The garden posts its own hours, including earlier entry for members. Buy tickets on the garden’s site. Free roses do not get you in.

Suits people who want a designed garden and will pay for it. Trade-off: it spends the same daylight as the roses and the zoo, and the three do not share a ticket.

Oregon Zoo

4001 SW Canyon Road. Hours change by season, and the zoo says the last timed entry is typically an hour before closing. Every guest, including children under 2, has to be on the reservation. Buy tickets on the zoo’s site. The zoo takes cards, not cash, and has cash-to-card machines inside.

Suits a family day. Trade-off: a timed entry and a separate walk inside the park. On a short day, choose the zoo or the gardens.

The west hills

Forest Park

Portland Parks lists 5,200 acres, more than 80 miles of trails, and more than 40 ways in. Parking is free and often tiny. TriMet lines 15, 26, and 77 serve the Lower Macleay trailhead and also Leif Erikson at Thurman, Aspen, and Lower Holman. Line 16 serves the Linnton trailhead, Lower Firelane 1, and the Lower Newton/BPA trailheads. Read the trail-closures page first. Rain and wind leave trees down and slides on the paths.

Suits a half day of walking that is not about the rose garden. Trade-off: the entrances are scattered, and this is not a quick add-on to downtown.

Pittock Mansion

3229 NW Pittock Drive. Admission is charged, and the hours on the visit page change with the season, so read that page rather than trusting a saved schedule. In summer the main lot is paid; the same page says you can use free gravel turnouts and walk up. Travel Portland connects the house to Northwest / Nob Hill and to Forest Park. Treat it as a west-hills stop.

Suits people who want the house and the terrace and do not mind a hill. Trade-off: a ticket, a small lot, and a detour from the river.

Across the river

OMSI

The Oregon Museum of Science and Industry is at 1945 SE Water Avenue. OMSI describes exhibits, a planetarium, and tours of the USS Blueback submarine. Shows and the submarine are extra, not part of general admission. The streetcar stops at OMSI. Hours and tickets are on the museum’s site.

Suits a wet day, and families. Trade-off: you are off the downtown side of the river. The streetcar links OMSI to the center, but it is not a five-minute walk from Powell’s.

A neighborhood street instead of another ticket

If the center has been enough, switch neighborhoods rather than adding a third museum. Alberta Street, in Northeast, is murals, shops, and places to eat. In summer, Last Thursday closes about fifteen blocks to traffic from 6 to 9 p.m. on the last Thursday of the month. Travel Portland also calls it about a fifteen-minute drive from the airport, which is a reason to put it at the start or the end of a trip, not in the middle of a downtown day.

Hawthorne Boulevard, in Southeast, runs 43 blocks of shops and cafes once you cross the Hawthorne Bridge. It has its own Powell’s. Division/Clinton, just south of Hawthorne, is the street Travel Portland describes as one of the city’s dining stretches. None of these three is on the streetcar. Use TriMet’s planner for the bus.

Old Town Chinatown, beside downtown, is where Travel Portland places Lan Su Chinese Garden and the Saturday Market. This guide does not repeat hours or a ticket price for the garden, because those were not confirmed on the garden’s own site. Look them up before you plan the morning around it.

Getting around

TriMet is the bus and MAX system. You pay by tapping a contactless card or phone, or a Hop card, on the reader as you board. TriMet’s how-to-ride page lists a $2.80 adult fare for that tap. Frequent Service buses run every 15 minutes or better through the day, every day. MAX runs every 15 minutes or less most of the day.

The MAX Red Line connects Portland International Airport with the city center, then continues toward Beaverton and Hillsboro. TriMet says the ride between the airport and downtown is about 40 minutes, $2.80 for adults 18 to 64, and that the airport station is near baggage claim. The first and last trains are listed on that same Red Line page, which matters if your flight is very early or very late.

A car is useful for a Forest Park trailhead the bus does not reach, and for Pittock if you do not want the walk up from the gravel. It is a liability in Washington Park on a sunny weekend.
