A weekend in Portland

Two nights covers the center and Washington Park. The third day is a choice, not another museum.

Two nights is the right length if you want downtown and Washington Park without rushing both into one day. A third day is for Forest Park, OMSI, or a neighborhood street. It is not for a third museum in the same part of town.

The days below assume you are sleeping downtown, in the Pearl, or in Northwest, where the streetcar and MAX do the work. If you are on Hawthorne or Alberta, keep the downtown day, and use TriMet’s planner for the ride in. Read where to stay in Portland before you book, and things to do for the details behind each stop.

Before you land

The MAX Red Line runs from Portland International Airport to the city center in about 40 minutes. TriMet lists a $2.80 adult fare and puts the station near baggage claim. Tap a contactless card or phone, or a Hop card, on the reader. If the flight is late, check the last train before you assume the Red Line is still running.

The art museum is closed Monday. If Monday is one of your days, put the museum on Saturday or Sunday.

Day 1: the center, on foot

Leave the bags and stay on the downtown side of the river.

  • Start at Pioneer Courthouse Square. It is a full block, a MAX stop, and an easy place to get oriented.
  • Walk the South Park Blocks to the Portland Art Museum at 1219 SW Park Avenue, if it is open. The historical society is on the same green stretch if you want a second indoor stop. Check hours before you promise the group a museum morning.
  • Drop down to Tom McCall Waterfront Park and walk as much of the 1.5 miles as you feel like. Salmon Street Springs and the Japanese American Historical Plaza are the named stops. Cross a bridge if you want the Eastbank Esplanade on the other bank. You do not need to walk both sides.
  • End at Powell’s City of Books, 1005 W Burnside, which lists regular hours of 10 a.m. to 9 p.m. It is large enough to fill whatever daylight you have left. The streetcar is there if your feet are done.

If you want a neighborhood after dark instead of another downtown block, Alberta’s restaurants are the move when you are willing to ride. On a summer Last Thursday, that ride is the evening plan: the street closes for about fifteen blocks from 6 to 9 p.m. On an ordinary night, Hawthorne or Division is the same idea, a long street of places to eat across the river. Do not try to do Alberta and the rose garden on day 1.

Day 2: Washington Park, by train

Take the MAX Red or Blue line to Washington Park station. Parking in the park is paid and, on a sunny weekend, the city says it is very limited. The free seasonal shuttle, or TriMet Line 63, links the station to the gardens. Confirm the shuttle on Explore Washington Park that morning.

  • International Rose Test Garden, 400 SW Kingston Avenue. Admission is free. The bloom runs from late May through October. If you are here in winter, the view and the formal beds are still there; the flowers are not the point.
  • Portland Japanese Garden, 611 SW Kingston, only if you want a second garden and a ticket. It is not included with the roses. Check the day’s hours. Members get an earlier window.
  • The Oregon Zoo, 4001 SW Canyon Road, only if the day is about animals. Buy a timed ticket ahead. Every person in the group, including infants, has to be on it. Do not add the zoo after both gardens unless you truly have the afternoon.

A clean version of this day is roses, then either the Japanese Garden or the zoo, then back on MAX. Hoyt Arboretum is in the same park if the group would rather walk among trees than see a third attraction. It does not need a ticket. You will still be dealing with the same park transportation.

Day 3: pick one direction

Use the third day for the thing day 2 did not cover. Do not invent a second pass through downtown.

Woods and a view

Forest Park is 5,200 acres and more than 80 miles of trail, with parking that is free and small. Lines 15, 26, and 77 reach the Lower Macleay trailhead. Check closures before you go. Pittock Mansion, at 3229 NW Pittock Drive, is the housed version of the same hills: a ticket, seasonal hours on the visit page, and a walk up if you skip the paid main lot. One of these is a full morning. Both is a long day.

Indoors, especially with children

OMSI, 1945 SE Water Avenue, is the science museum, with a planetarium and USS Blueback submarine tours sold separately. The streetcar stops there. It is the right third day when the weather is bad or the zoo was the thing you skipped.

A street, and then the airport

If day 3 is also departure day, do not start a hike. Hawthorne or Alberta in the morning, then the Red Line from downtown, is the calmer ending. Alberta is the one Travel Portland puts about fifteen minutes from the airport by car, which helps only if you are not relying on MAX. If you are on the train, go back through downtown and board the Red Line there.

If you only have two days

Do day 1 and day 2, in that order. Downtown first means a bad-weather day is already spent indoors, and the park day can move if the forecast is awful. Drop the Japanese Garden or the zoo, not the roses, unless the trip is specifically for the zoo. Leave Forest Park for another visit.
