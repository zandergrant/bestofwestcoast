Oregon · Portland

Where to stay in Portland

Downtown if you want the airport train and the museums. A neighborhood street if you want the evenings. The trade-off is the ride between them.

Where you sleep in Portland changes the trip more than which downtown hotel brand you pick. Downtown has the hotels, the airport train, and the museums. The neighborhoods on the east side have the long walking streets, and a bus ride back to the center. Northwest has the old houses and the way up to Forest Park. None of these is a bad base. They fail when the neighborhood and the days you planned do not match.

This guide does not name individual hotels. Travel Portland’s neighborhood pages link out to hotel lists and rental sites. A specific property belongs in a later guide, checked on that hotel’s own site.

Downtown, if this is the first trip

Travel Portland calls downtown walkable, with shorter blocks than a typical American downtown, and says it has the city’s highest concentration of hotels: more than 35, from larger hotels to smaller inns. The art museum, Pioneer Courthouse Square, Tom McCall Waterfront Park, and the MAX stop for the airport are here. The Portland Streetcar runs through downtown if you want to ride instead of walk.

The MAX Red Line is the airport link. TriMet says the trip is about 40 minutes, the adult fare is $2.80, and the airport station is near baggage claim. That is the practical reason to stay downtown when you are not renting a car. Check the first and last trains on the Red Line schedule if the flight is early or late. A downtown room does not help if the train has stopped.

Suits a first visit, a trip built around the museum and the river, and anyone coming from the airport on MAX. Trade-off: you are in the busiest part of the city. The long neighborhood streets, Alberta and Hawthorne, are a ride away, not out the front door. Oregon has no general sales tax on shopping, which Travel Portland points out; it does not make downtown quiet.

The Pearl, if you want downtown without sleeping on the mall

Travel Portland describes the Pearl as a former warehouse district of galleries, shops, and restaurants, with loading docks and cobblestones still in the streetscape. A stay here puts you next to downtown, Old Town Chinatown, and Northwest. Powell’s City of Books is at 1005 W Burnside, on the Pearl’s edge. The streetcar runs on 10th and 11th and along Lovejoy, so you are on the line rather than hoping for a bus.

Suits people who want to walk to Powell’s and still reach the art museum and the river without a car. Trade-off: the airport MAX is a downtown connection, not a station at the hotel door. You ride the streetcar or walk to a MAX stop. The Pearl is also more galleries and lofts than the civic center. If you want the waterfront path as your morning walk, downtown is the more direct base.

Northwest / Nob Hill, if the park matters

Travel Portland describes Northwest / Nob Hill as Victorian houses turned into shops and restaurants, with a route toward Forest Park and Pittock Mansion. The streetcar stops at NW 23rd and Marshall, so the shopping street is on the line.

Suits a trip that includes Forest Park or the mansion, and people who would rather wander a neighborhood street than the hotel core. Trade-off: you are up off the river. The art museum and Tom McCall Waterfront Park are a streetcar ride or a longer walk back toward downtown. The hills start as you head into the park. Do not pick Nob Hill for a trip that is mostly OMSI and the east side.

Alberta, if you want a northeast street and you have a flight

The Alberta Arts District is in Northeast: murals, shops, galleries, and a lot of places to eat. Black heritage markers run along the sidewalk between Northeast 11th and Northeast 24th. In summer, Last Thursday closes about fifteen blocks from 6 to 9 p.m. Travel Portland describes it as northeast of downtown and about a fifteen-minute drive from the airport.

The visitor bureau points visitors to vacation rentals as well as lodging on the street. This is not the hotel core. The streetcar does not run on Alberta. Buses do; plan them in TriMet’s trip planner rather than assuming a short hop to the museum.

Suits a second visit, or a first visit that cares more about a neighborhood street than about stacking the rose garden and the art museum. It also suits a tight airport connection if you are fine with a car or a rideshare for that fifteen-minute airport hop. Trade-off: Powell’s flagship, the art museum, and the waterfront are no longer walkable. Washington Park is a real trek. You gain the street and give up the cluster of downtown sights.

Hawthorne or Division, if you want the southeast streets

Hawthorne Boulevard is across the Hawthorne Bridge from downtown. Travel Portland describes 43 walkable blocks of shops, cafes, and restaurants, and a similar feel on Belmont a few blocks north. Powell’s has a store at 3723 SE Hawthorne Boulevard. Division/Clinton, just south, is the district the bureau describes as one of the city’s main dining streets.

Suits people who want a long commercial street, coffee, and dinner plans, and who will cross the river for the museum day. Trade-off: you are not on the streetcar or the airport MAX. A bus or a rideshare gets you downtown. Do not stay here if the trip is a single full day and the rose garden is the point. Do stay here if you already know you want two dinners on the east side and one day in the center.

Lloyd, if the trip is a convention or a game

Travel Portland places the Oregon Convention Center and the Moda Center, home of the Portland Trail Blazers, in the Lloyd district, just across the river in Northeast. The streetcar stops at the Convention Center, and MAX serves this side of the river on the way to the airport.

Suits a convention, a game, or a concert, when the venue matters more than a neighborhood street. Trade-off: it is an events district. You can ride to downtown, but you will not walk out into a 43-block shopping street. Alberta and Hawthorne are better if the evenings are the point and you have no ticket to an arena.

What to skip as a base

Washington Park looks central on a attractions list and is a poor place to stay around unless you have a specific reason. Parking is paid and tight, and the gardens close on their own schedules. Sleep downtown or in the Pearl and ride MAX.

The airport itself is the right base only when the flight forces it. The Red Line’s last trains are on the TriMet page. If you can make that train, a downtown room is the more useful night.
