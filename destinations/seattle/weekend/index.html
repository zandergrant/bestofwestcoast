Washington · Seattle

A weekend in Seattle

Two days for the center of the city. The third day is a boat or the locks, not a third ticket.

Two days covers the market, the waterfront, and Seattle Center. A third day is the ferry or the locks, not a third ticket at the center. If you are staying downtown, in Belltown, or on Capitol Hill, the first two days do not need a car. Ballard and the locks do not sit on the 1 Line. Read where to stay with that in mind, and things to do for hours and rules.

From the airport

The Sound Transit 1 Line stops at SeaTac/Airport and at Westlake, Capitol Hill, Pioneer Square, and Stadium. That is the arrival if your room is downtown or on Capitol Hill. If the room is in Queen Anne or Ballard, the train gets you to downtown and then you still have a ride. Check the last train if the flight is late.

Day 1: market and waterfront

  • Pike Place Market, 85 Pike Street. Go earlier than you think. The middle of the day is the crowded version. The market posts seasonal street-access rules; glance at them so you are not surprised by a closure.
  • Walk the waterfront north to Olympic Sculpture Park, 2901 Western Avenue. It is free, open from half an hour before sunrise to half an hour after sunset, and about a mile north of the Seattle Art Museum. Do not plan on the garage unless you drove. The park is the walk.
  • Stop there. A Needle ticket on the same day is how both end up rushed. Save Seattle Center for day 2, when you can buy one ticket and actually use it.

Dinner can stay downtown or move to whatever neighborhood you slept in. Capitol Hill is a train ride. Belltown is a walk back from the park.

Day 2: Seattle Center, and one view

Seattle Center is at 305 Harrison Street. Pick one paid attraction. The Space Needle is at 400 Broad Street. Its site lists hours by day and a general-admission range that was $35 to $55 when checked on October 28, 2026. Chihuly Garden and Glass and the Museum of Pop Culture are the other names Visit Seattle groups here. Each has its own ticket. Doing all three is a day with no room in it.

  • Walk or ride the monorail, which Visit Seattle mentions as the way to the center. Confirm the monorail that morning.
  • If you want the skyline without the ticket, go up to Kerry Park on Queen Anne instead of, or after, the Needle. Visit Seattle sends people there for the view. The hill is the cost. Do not do Kerry Park, the Needle, Chihuly, and MoPOP.
  • Arena nights at Climate Pledge Arena fill Lower Queen Anne. If you do not have a ticket, eat somewhere that is not trying to serve the crowd.

Day 3: water, or the locks

Choose one.

Bainbridge on the ferry

Washington State Ferries sails between Seattle and Bainbridge Island, and also between Seattle and Bremerton. Walk on. Leave the car in Seattle if you have one; the vehicle line is a different project, with its own reservations. Check the day’s sailings and fares on the WSDOT site, and pick a return before you wander off on the other side. Bainbridge is the shorter, more usual walk-on. Bremerton is the one to choose only if the schedule that day is actually better.

The locks, if you would rather stay ashore

The Hiram M. Chittenden Locks are at 3015 NW 54th Street in Ballard. Grounds are free, 7 a.m. to 9 p.m., with a fish ladder and boats moving between the lakes and Puget Sound. Parking is paid on the Corps’ posted schedule, except Sundays and holidays. The 1 Line will not take you there. Go only if you are willing to spend the middle of the day on the trip. It pairs well with a Ballard lunch and poorly with a 4 p.m. flight.

If you only have two days

Day 1 and day 2. Skip the ferry. You can see the Sound from the sculpture park and from the Needle’s line of sight without spending the departure day on a boat.

A stadium weekend

If the reason for the trip is a game at Lumen Field or T-Mobile Park, stay near Pioneer Square or ride the 1 Line to Stadium or Pioneer Square, and do the market on the other day. Do not put the locks between you and a first pitch.
