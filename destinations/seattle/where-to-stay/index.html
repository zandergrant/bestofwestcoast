Washington · Seattle

Where to stay in Seattle

The 1 Line runs from the airport through downtown and Capitol Hill. Queen Anne and Ballard are good for different trips, and they are off that train.

Stay on the Sound Transit 1 Line if you are not renting a car. That train runs from the airport through downtown and Capitol Hill, and it does not go to Ballard, Fremont, or Queen Anne. Those neighborhoods are good bases for different trips. They are annoying bases for a first weekend of the market, the waterfront, and Seattle Center.

This guide does not name hotels. Visit Seattle’s lodging page will point you at properties. The decision here is the neighborhood.

Downtown, for a first visit

Visit Seattle describes downtown as the retail and restaurant core, with Pike Place Market in it. Westlake is a 1 Line station, so the airport train and the market are in the same part of town. The waterfront and, a mile north, the Olympic Sculpture Park are the walks.

Suits a first trip, a short trip, and anyone landing at SeaTac who wants to ride the 1 Line and start walking. Trade-off: you are in the middle of the visitor city. It is convenient and it is busy. You will not feel like you stumbled into a side street. If that is what you wanted, look at Capitol Hill or Ballard and accept the ride back.

Belltown, if you want the north edge of downtown

Visit Seattle places Belltown just north of downtown, a mix of towers and places to eat. That puts you toward the sculpture park at 2901 Western Avenue and within a walk of Seattle Center, and a bit farther from Pioneer Square and the stadiums.

Suits people who want downtown’s transit without sleeping on top of the market, and who expect to end the day at Seattle Center or the park. Trade-off: Pioneer Square, the stadiums, and the International District are no longer the nearest neighborhood. Event nights pull the other direction.

Lower Queen Anne, for Seattle Center

Visit Seattle splits Queen Anne in two. Lower Queen Anne has Seattle Center: the Space Needle at 400 Broad Street, MoPOP, Chihuly Garden and Glass, McCaw Hall, and Climate Pledge Arena. You can walk or take the monorail to the center. Upper Queen Anne is the hill of older houses, with Kerry Park as the viewpoint the bureau sends people up to see.

Suits a trip that is actually about the Needle, a game, or a show, if you stay in the lower part. Upper Queen Anne suits people who want the view and a quieter street and will deal with the hill. Trade-off: the 1 Line does not stop here. You are close to the center and one move away from the airport train. On event nights the lower neighborhood is busy because the arena is right there. The upper hill is the trade: better view, more climbing, farther from dinner density downtown.

Capitol Hill, if you want a neighborhood on the train

The 1 Line stops at Capitol Hill, between the University of Washington and Westlake. Visit Seattle describes tree-lined streets, older houses, and commercial streets, and it is the neighborhood the bureau tells people to go out in. You can be at the airport, downtown, or a stadium without a car.

Suits a car-free trip that still wants somewhere to go at night, and a second visit that found downtown too plain. Trade-off: Pike Place is a train ride, not a walk. Seattle Center is not next door the way it is in Lower Queen Anne. You are choosing evenings and the train over rolling out of bed into the market.

Pioneer Square, for a game

Visit Seattle calls Pioneer Square the original neighborhood and places it next to Lumen Field and T-Mobile Park. The 1 Line stops at Pioneer Square and at Stadium.

Suits a trip built around a game or a show in that district. Trade-off: event days fill the sidewalks. It is a weaker base if you have no ticket and wanted a quiet morning. Downtown or Capitol Hill is easier when the stadiums are not the point.

Ballard, if the neighborhood is the trip

Visit Seattle describes Ballard as a maritime neighborhood and a place for eating. The locks are at its north edge, at 3015 NW 54th Street, and the grounds are free to walk. The 1 Line does not go there.

Suits people who want a local center, the locks, and dinner more than they want the Needle. Trade-off: every trip to the market, the waterfront, or the airport train takes longer. Do not stay in Ballard for a one-night stopover. Do stay there for a longer visit when you are done treating downtown as the whole city.

A note on Fremont

Visit Seattle calls Fremont, on the ship canal, the self-proclaimed center of the universe, and describes it as its own small scene. Like Ballard, it is off the 1 Line. Visit it. Do not make it the hotel for a first weekend unless you already like the idea of a bus.
