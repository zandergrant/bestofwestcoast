Oregon · Cannon Beach

Where to stay in Cannon Beach

Stay where you can walk to the beach and to dinner. Drive when you want the state park.

Cannon Beach is about three miles of town against four miles of beach. The visitor bureau’s lodging page says you can park once, even though spaces are limited, and walk most of what you came for. That is the whole lodging decision: sleep where you can walk to the sand and to dinner, and drive only when you want Ecola State Park.

The same pages describe resorts, boutique hotels, vacation rentals, and campgrounds, including oceanfront rooms. This guide does not name properties. Pick the area first, then use the bureau’s lodging list and the property’s own site.

In town, on foot

Staying in town is the right default. Haystack Rock, the beach, shops, and restaurants are the walk. Parking is free and limited, so a room that lets you leave the car is worth more than a room that looks central on a map and still requires a hunt for a stall every time you come back.

The bureau says to reserve ahead, especially on holidays, in summer, and on event weekends. The town is small. When it is full, it is full. A weeknight outside those peaks is when you can be pickier.

Suits a weekend without much driving, couples who want dinner and the beach, and families who will spend the day on the sand. Trade-off: Ecola State Park is not out the door. From 5th Street it is 1.5 miles up Ecola Park Road to the fee station, then farther to Indian Beach. You will use the car for the park, and you will pay the day-use fee there. In June through September the park lot lines up. Sleeping in town does not reserve you a parking spot at the viewpoint.

Oceanfront, if the view is the reason

The bureau advertises oceanfront and ocean-view rooms alongside the in-town lodgings. An oceanfront room puts the beach at the door, which matters in a town where the weather can pin you inside for part of a day. You can watch the rock without timing a walk.

Suits people who came for the shore and will pay for it, and anyone who wants the morning to start on sand rather than on a street. Trade-off: the shops and restaurants are still in town, so an oceanfront room at the far end of those three miles is a longer walk to dinner. In summer you are also on the busiest stretch of sand. If walking to a restaurant matters as much as the view, stay closer to the middle of town and accept that the water may be a few blocks away.

A rental or a campground, if the group is larger

The bureau lists vacation rentals of different sizes, and campgrounds for RVs and tents, alongside hotels. A rental is the practical answer when you need a kitchen or more than one room. A campground is the answer when the night outside is the point.

Trade-off for an RV: do not plan to take it up to Ecola. State parks says motorhomes and trucks with trailers are not recommended on that road, and overnight parking in the park is prohibited. Camp in town or at a campground that can take the rig, and drive a smaller vehicle to the park, or skip the park. Hiker’s Camp in Ecola is for people walking the Oregon Coast Trail. It is not a place to park and sleep.

Without a car

You can reach Cannon Beach by bus. The visitor bureau lists Northwest POINT twice a day from Portland, with tickets through Greyhound and Amtrak, and Sunset Empire Transportation District for local north-coast routes. Once you are in town, the beach and the shops work on foot.

Suits a short stay that is about the rock, the sand, and dinner. Trade-off: Ecola’s narrow entrance road is the thing you give up, or you spend the day solving it. Do not assume a local bus drops you at the viewpoint. Check Sunset Empire’s map before you promise anyone Indian Beach. The North by Northwest Connector passes, listed at $25 for three days and $30 for seven, are a cash, exact-change product as of this check. Confirm them if the bus is your only plan.

What the weather does to the room

The bureau’s planning page describes summer highs in the upper 60s and winter lows in the high 30s, with the wettest months from November through January. An oceanfront room in July is still a sweater room in the evening. A winter weekend is a storm-watching trip, which is a good use of an oceanfront window and a bad time to plan a long hike on a trail the park may have closed for a slide. Read the Ecola alerts if the bluff is half the reason you booked.
