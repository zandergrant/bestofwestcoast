Oregon · Cannon Beach

A weekend in Cannon Beach

Time the rock to the tide. Go to Ecola in the morning, before the summer line.

A Cannon Beach weekend is two nights and three unhurried days, or two days if you are only coming for the rock and one dinner. The order matters because of the tide and because Ecola’s parking fills on summer afternoons. Read things to do for the rules at the rock and the park, and where to stay before you book a room at the wrong end of town.

Getting in

From Portland, the visitor bureau’s route is Highway 26 west to US 101, then four miles south. The same page calls it less than two hours and about 80 miles. Northwest POINT is listed twice a day from Portland if you are not driving. Confirm that schedule. You want the car if Ecola is on the trip.

Check the tide before you lock day 1. The useful time at Haystack Rock is an hour or more before low tide. If the low tide is early, do the rock first and the town after. If it is late, reverse them. Do not put the rock in the middle of a high tide because that is when you happen to arrive.

Day 1: the rock, then the town

  • Park once. Spaces in town are free and limited. A room in town lets you leave the car.
  • Walk the beach to Haystack Rock in the low-tide window. Stay on sand and bare rock, take nothing, and do not climb above the barnacle line. The rock is a wildlife refuge and a marine garden. Puffins are a spring and early-summer reason to bring binoculars, not a reason to scramble up.
  • Leave the beach while you are still glad to be there. Nearly four miles of sand sounds short until the wind picks up.
  • Use the rest of the day in town. Book the dinner you care about. The bureau says the restaurants get busy in summer and on event weekends, and that some keep short hours in the quiet season.

Skip Ecola on the arrival day unless you landed early and the park lot is still open. Combining a travel day, the tide, and the bluff is how you miss the tide.

Day 2: Ecola, earlier than you want

Go in the morning. From June through September the bureau says lines form, and the lots at Ecola Point and Indian Beach are limited. From 5th Street, drive 1.5 miles north on Ecola Park Road to the fee station. A day-use permit is required. State parks says to call for the current fee. Left for Ecola Point, right for two miles to Indian Beach.

  • Do not take an RV or a trailer. The road is tight, and there is little room to turn around. Overnight parking in the park is prohibited, so this is a day trip from your room, not a place to sleep.
  • Ecola Point is the view south over the sea stacks and the town, and the start of trails. Indian Beach is the cove, the surf, and more tidepools.
  • If you want a hike, use the Oregon Coast Trail segment or the Clatsop Loop, and read the alerts first. The west section of the Clatsop Loop has been closed since a landslide notice dated April 17, 2026. The headland slides. A trail that was open last year may not be open now.
  • Pets in the park need a leash no longer than six feet. Drones are not allowed.

Come back to town for a slower afternoon. You already did the rock. The second beach walk can be aimless.

Day 3: more beach, or the bus home

If you have a third day, do not add a new town you have not looked up. The beach from downtown is long enough for another morning, and the weather is the entertainment. Summer highs sit in the upper 60s on the bureau’s description, and November through January are the wet months. Pack layers even if Portland was warm when you left.

If you came by bus, day 3 is the ride back. POINT is listed twice daily. Do not sightsee up the Ecola road on the morning you have a bus to catch. If you have a car and an early departure, the rock at dawn is the better goodbye than a second attempt at the park lot.

If you only have two days

Day 1 is the tide and the town. Day 2 is Ecola in the morning and the drive home after lunch. You will not be bored. You will be cold if you packed for a heat wave.
