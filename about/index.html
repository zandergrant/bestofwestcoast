About and editorial standards.

Best of West Coast covers California, Oregon, and Washington. A finished guide helps a traveler decide. This page is the rulebook for what can go on the site.

The site you are reading is the first public version, updated October 28, 2026. It holds the coverage map, the category hubs, these standards, and a places database with empty recommendation fields. Published guides: none yet.

Quality rules

  1. Geography stays fixed.

    California, Oregon, and Washington are the map. Lake Tahoe is in scope on the California side. The Columbia River Gorge is in scope where it lies in Oregon or Washington.

  2. A recommendation explains itself.

    A published pick says who it suits and what you give up by choosing it. The reasoning is part of the piece.

  3. Facts that can go stale need a source and a date.

    Hours, prices, safety, accessibility, and transportation are published with a cited source and the date an editor checked them.

  4. Reviews come from the editorial process.

    Star ratings and review quotes are published when this publication produced them. Invented scores stay out.

  5. First person means the writer went.

    A first-person travel story is published when the writer took the trip.

  6. Photographs are of the place.

    Pictures are licensed photographs. Generated images are illustrations, and they are labeled as illustrations. They are not presented as photographs of a destination.

  7. A person approves the page.

    Research and drafts may be AI-assisted. An editor approves accuracy, the distinctions, and the images before publication. The site is not an automatic content mill.

These rules are binding. A page that cannot meet them stays labeled Guide coming soon.

How a guide is made

  1. Start from a decision travelers have to make, inside the three-state map.
  2. Read official sources and independent reporting. Keep the citations.
  3. Update the place record: attributes, sources, image rights, and the verification date.
  4. Draft the guide, including why one option is separated from another, and link it to related guides.
  5. An editor checks the facts, whether the distinctions are useful, and whether the images are licensed.
  6. Publish, then come back when the facts go stale.

Affiliates and ads

Hotel and activity links are the planned commercial path. A paid or affiliate relationship will be labeled next to the link. This version has no booking links and no advertisers.

The letter

The West Coast Five is the weekly newsletter: one stay, one place to eat, one activity, one weekend, and one trip to plan. It is not being sent yet.
