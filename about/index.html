About

How we publish

Short guides, checked before they go up. The blend formula was written October 29, 2026.

Best of West Coast is a set of guides for travelers. A finished one helps you choose. If a guide is not ready, the page says so.

What a guide has to clear

  1. The West Coast.

    California, Oregon, and Washington. Lake Tahoe means the California shore. The Columbia Gorge means the stretch in Oregon and Washington.

  2. A choice, with the trade-off.

    A recommendation says who it suits and what you give up by picking it.

  3. Facts that can go stale, with a source.

    Hours, prices, safety, access, and how to get somewhere appear with a source and the date we checked them.

  4. Reviews we actually made.

    We don’t publish invented scores, star ratings, or quotes from visits that didn’t happen.

  5. First person means someone went.

    A story in the first person is from a writer who took the trip.

  6. Pictures of real places.

    A photograph is a photograph of that place. Drawings on the site are drawings.

  7. A person says yes.

    Research and drafts may be written with help from software. A person approves the facts, the distinctions, and the pictures before a guide goes up.

Until a guide clears that, it stays marked coming soon.

How a blended score is made

The Best directory puts one number next to the separate scores from Google, Yelp, Tripadvisor, and any other site whose page we could open. The number is not a guess, and it is not a plain average.

Each review pulls in proportion to how many people wrote it. A 4.6 from 5,000 reviews outweighs a 5.0 from 12. Before that math, we add 100 stand-in reviews at 4.0. Four is a middle score on these sites. The stand-in reviews keep a tiny perfect score from jumping the line.

The formula is (100 × 4.0 + each score times its review count) divided by (100 + the total number of reviews). A 5.0 from 12 reviews lands near 4.11. A 4.6 from 5,000 lands near 4.59. We round the result to two decimals, half up.

If a site scores out of 10, we divide that score by 2 before it enters the blend, and we still show the original number. We do not invent a score for a site we could not open. The same guest may have written reviews on more than one site. We do not try to remove those duplicates. The per-site numbers stay on the card so you can ignore the blend.

How a guide gets written

  1. Start from a choice a traveler actually has to make.
  2. Read the sources and keep the citations.
  3. Write the comparison, including the trade-off.
  4. An editor checks it. Then we publish, and we come back when the facts go stale.

Notes on each place

We keep one set of notes for each place, and we leave the details blank until they’ve been checked. The file is here, if you want to see the blanks.
